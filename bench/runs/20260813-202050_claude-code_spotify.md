# claude-code — 25 songs

1. **Jimpster — Dangly Panther** (2006)  
   _Jimpster runs Freerange, the label that put out Milton Jackson and Shur-I-Kan — this is the house end of that roster, same warm Rhodes chords and shuffling live-feel drums._
2. **Isolée — Beau Mot Plage** (1998)  
   _The record that made microhouse a genre — clipped guitar loops over a dubbed-out shuffle, the same fascination with texture-as-groove that runs through the Milton Jackson tracks._
3. **Kerri Chandler — Rain** (2007)  
   _The deep-house vocal lineage Kings Of Tomorrow come out of — Chandler is the New Jersey source for that whole warm, gospel-inflected sound._
4. **Âme — Rej** (2005)  
   _The track that pulled deep house back toward melody in the mid-2000s — a single rolling bassline and one hook, held for eight minutes. Sits right beside the Milton Jackson material._
5. **Motor City Drum Ensemble — Raw Cuts #3** (2009)  
   _Danilo Plessow builds house out of chopped jazz and soul records, which is exactly the bridge between your St Germain and your Milton Jackson tracks._
6. **Nosaj Thing — Aquarium** (2009)  
   _Core Los Angeles beat scene — the same Low End Theory circle as Flying Lotus and Ras G, but sparser, with a lot of space left between the drums._
7. **TOKiMONSTA — Lucid Waking** (2010)  
   _Signed to Flying Lotus's Brainfeeder and a Low End Theory regular — the same swung, off-grid drum programming as your Teebs and Ras G tracks._
8. **Hudson Mohawke — FUSE** (2009)  
   _The Glasgow answer to the LA beat scene — maximalist where Teebs is minimal, but the same warped-sample, wrong-speed sensibility as your Lone tracks._
9. **Machinedrum — Gunshotta** (2013)  
   _Footwork tempos wrapped in jungle breaks and pads — the natural next step from the Lone records, which chase the same rave nostalgia at a lower BPM._
10. **Clams Casino — I'm God** (2011)  
   _Blown-out, pitch-shifted vocal samples over cavernous reverb — the instrumental hip hop side of the same cloud that produced your oOoOO and Purity Ring tracks._
11. **Balam Acab — See Birds (Moon)** (2011)  
   _Tri Angle Records labelmate of oOoOO — the same pitched-down vocals and water-logged reverb, but built on field recordings rather than drum machines._
12. **Com Truise — Brokendate** (2011)  
   _Deliberately degraded 80s synth textures at half speed — the polished end of the same nostalgia your Home tracks run on._
13. **Salvia Palth — i was all over her** (2013)  
   _Bedroom recording that became a slow-burn internet standard — the same lo-fi, tape-saturated intimacy that made the Home and Foxes In Fiction tracks land._
14. **Bonobo — Kiara** (2010)  
   _Simon Green remixed the Maya Jane Coles track in your playlist — this is his own work, the same live-instrument downtempo that connects your Thievery Corporation and Quantic picks._
15. **Glassjaw — Tip Your Bartender** (2002)  
   _The post-hardcore record Deftones fans always end up at — same dynamic swing between whispered restraint and full-band detonation, with a far more elastic rhythm section._
16. **Quicksand — Fazer** (1993)  
   _The New York post-hardcore blueprint that Deftones and Glassjaw both built on — dense, mid-tempo, and rhythmically heavier than it is loud._
17. **Far — Mother Mary** (1998)  
   _Sacramento contemporaries of Deftones, and the missing link to Incubus's melodic side — Jonah Matranga sings where the genre expected a scream._
18. **Chevelle — Send the Pain Below** (2002)  
   _Exact contemporaries of Pressure 4-5 working the same drop-tuned, tightly produced early-2000s alt-metal, but with more melodic lift in the chorus._
19. **Unknown Mortal Orchestra — So Good at Being in Trouble** (2013)  
   _Ruban Nielson records everything himself through deliberately cheap gear, which gives it the same slightly-wrong, sun-bleached quality as the Tame Impala tracks._
20. **Pond — Sweep Me Off My Feet** (2017)  
   _Shares members with Tame Impala and is produced by Kevin Parker — literally the same Perth studio and the same hands on the desk, pushed further toward glam._
21. **Built to Spill — Car** (1994)  
   _The Pacific Northwest indie record Modest Mouse grew up on — same angular, unresolved guitar lines and the same refusal to land on an obvious chorus._
22. **Grizzly Bear — Two Weeks** (2009)  
   _Four-part harmony over a deliberately stiff piano figure — the same interest in odd arrangement and layered vocals that makes the Menomena track work._
23. **Souls of Mischief — 93 'til Infinity** (1993)  
   _The Bay Area answer to the Native Tongues records in your playlist — jazz loops, interlocking four-man verses, and no interest in menace._
24. **Black Sheep — The Choice Is Yours (Revisited)** (1991)  
   _Actual Native Tongues members — the same collective that produced De La Soul and A Tribe Called Quest, working the same loose, funny, sample-heavy formula._
25. **Digable Planets — Rebirth of Slick (Cool Like Dat)** (1993)  
   _Built on an Art Blakey sample and delivered in a cool, conversational cadence — the jazz-rap counterpart to your Brubeck and Pharcyde picks in one track._
