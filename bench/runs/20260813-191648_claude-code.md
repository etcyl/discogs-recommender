# claude-code — 25 songs

1. **Harmonia — Watussi** (1974)  
   _Michael Rother's guitar from Neu! meets Cluster's synth pastoral — the same motorik pulse as 'Hallogallo' but softened into something almost pastoral._
2. **Cluster — Hollywood** (1974)  
   _Conny Plank's production gives the drum machine the same dry, close-miked snap as the Neu! records, but the melodies are woozy and detuned in a way that anticipates Broadcast._
3. **Faust — Jennifer** (1973)  
   _The slow-motion organ drone and buried vocal sit exactly between Can's groove-based tracks and the narcotic haze of Slowdive — a krautrock record that already sounds like shoegaze._
4. **Manuel Göttsching — Sunrain** (1976)  
   _Sequencer arpeggios that pulse for eleven minutes without ever resolving — the missing link between Neu!'s forward motion and the generative patience of Eno's ambient records._
5. **The High Llamas — Checking In, Checking Out** (1996)  
   _Sean O'Hagan arranged and played on the Stereolab records this playlist draws from — here he does the same Bacharach-via-Brian-Wilson orchestration under his own name._
6. **Laika — Coming Down Glass** (1994)  
   _Too Pure labelmates of Stereolab, building loops out of found percussion and processed guitar — and the album title is a direct nod to the same Silver Apples lineage in this playlist._
7. **The Advisory Circle — As the Crow Flies** (2008)  
   _Ghost Box's whole project is the library-music melancholy Broadcast were mining — analogue synth, tape hiss, and the ghost of a 1970s public information film._
8. **Beth Gibbons & Rustin Man — Mysteries** (2002)  
   _The single most direct bridge in this playlist: Portishead's singer fronting Talk Talk's bassist, with the same close-miked acoustic restraint as 'New Grass'._
9. **Tricky — Aftermath** (1995)  
   _Tricky rapped on Massive Attack's first two albums before this; 'Aftermath' is the same Bristol dub-weight and smoke, but with the paranoia dialled up._
10. **Bark Psychosis — Big Shot** (1994)  
   _The record that got the phrase 'post-rock' coined, and it is audibly Talk Talk's 'Laughing Stock' method — long silences, live-room dynamics, jazz drumming under rock instrumentation._
11. **Disco Inferno — Second Language** (1994)  
   _A post-punk band who ran every instrument through samplers, so a guitar strum triggers breaking glass — Wire's terseness rebuilt with Aphex-era technology._
12. **Seefeel — Plainsong** (1993)  
   _The exact midpoint between the two poles of this playlist: My Bloody Valentine's guitar wash looped into Warp-label techno, released on Too Pure then Warp._
13. **Autechre — Eutow** (1995)  
   _The Warp peer to Boards of Canada and Aphex — same era, same label, but the rhythms are corroded and metallic rather than nostalgic._
14. **Flying Saucer Attack — My Dreaming Hill** (1995)  
   _Home-recorded feedback drone with an acoustic folk song buried inside — the same relationship between hiss and melody that Grouper works with, twenty years earlier._
15. **Stars of the Lid — Requiem for Dying Mothers, Pt. 1** (2001)  
   _Bowed-string drones that swell and decay on a glacial timescale — Harold Budd's harmonic language without the piano, and the closest living relative to 'An Ending (Ascent)'._
16. **Gas — Pop 1** (2000)  
   _Wolfgang Voigt buries an orchestral loop under a four-four kick and layers of tape fog — Boards of Canada's nostalgic murk applied to German romanticism._
17. **Laraaji — The Dance No. 1** (1980)  
   _Eno produced this as the third entry in his own Ambient series, and the hammered zither shimmer connects straight to Alice Coltrane's harp on 'Journey in Satchidananda'._
18. **Magazine — The Light Pours Out of Me** (1978)  
   _Martin Hannett produced this the same year he made 'Disorder' sound the way it does, and John McGeoch's icy chorused guitar is the template Robert Smith would build 'A Forest' on._
19. **Felt — Primitive Painters** (1985)  
   _Robin Guthrie produced it and Elizabeth Fraser sings the second voice — it is a Cocteau Twins record wearing a jangle-pop coat._
20. **The Chameleons — Second Skin** (1983)  
   _Twin chorus-drenched guitars over a driving eighth-note bassline — the exact machinery of 'A Forest' and 'Disorder', but pushed toward euphoria instead of dread._
21. **A.R. Kane — A Love from Outer Space** (1988)  
   _They coined 'dreampop' for what they were doing — guitar treated as pure texture over dub space, sitting exactly between Cocteau Twins and the free-jazz end of this playlist._
22. **Pere Ubu — Final Solution** (1976)  
   _Cleveland's answer to the CBGB scene that produced 'Marquee Moon' and 'Ghost Rider' — same year, same art-damaged rock, with an EML synth doing the screaming._
23. **Don Cherry — Brown Rice** (1975)  
   _Electric bass, tabla and whispered vocals over a modal vamp — the spiritual-jazz end of this playlist crossed with the electric texture of 'He Loved Him Madly'._
24. **Lonnie Liston Smith & the Cosmic Echoes — Expansions** (1975)  
   _Smith played Rhodes on Pharoah Sanders' 'Astral Traveling' and on Miles' 'On the Corner' sessions — this is the same player taking that cosmic Rhodes sound into his own name._
25. **Art Ensemble of Chicago — Theme de Yoyo** (1970)  
   _The AACM's answer to Sun Ra's Arkestra — costumed, theatrical, and here riding an actual funk bassline, which makes it the most immediately playable entry point into that world._
